The volatility flag uses the first available series among demand_units, yield, and tempC.
At each step it computes an ordinary rolling mean and sample standard deviation over the
last 20 observations, with one observation allowed during startup. With the benchmark's
15-minute data, 20 observations correspond to five hours. A point is labelled anomaly
when the absolute rolling z score is greater than 2.0; zero rolling variance gives z=0.
The code does not use a 30-day window, an exponentially weighted mean, or a seven-day
half-life.

At nominal intensity, the overproduction scenario multiplies inventory by 2.5 from hour
12 through hour 60 and adds 8*(1-exp(-0.3*(h-12))) degrees Celsius during that window.
After hour 60, the temperature addition decays as 8*exp(-0.15*(h-60)). It does not
represent a measured facility capacity limit or mandate a disposition route.

The adaptive_pricing scenario separately adds a 60-step sinusoid of amplitude 45 and a
keyed per-seed Normal(0,14) demand perturbation at nominal intensity. The volatility flag
tau contributes the declared logit vector [0.25, 0.05, -0.25]. It does not deterministically
mandate a route, change route distances, allocate safety stock, or create alert reports.

All coefficients are synthetic case-study assumptions. They are not estimated market
effects, forecasts of a real demand series, or regulatory risk-management requirements.
